# Manuscript blueprint (verified 2026-10-28)

The scaffold version of this file was generic text shared across groups: its related-work sentences described the six papers wrongly (for example 'Chen et al. evaluated multi-agent coordination'), it pointed to a model file that does not exist and it claimed 'paired t-tests, p < 0.001 across all metrics', which our results do not show. It is replaced by this blueprint. **The authoritative manuscript is `paper/paper.tex` (PDF: `paper/paper.pdf`, 4 pages, 3 figures, 2 tables, 5 citations).** Every number below is read from `results/guide_robot_test/` or `results/motion_validation/`.

**Title (authorised, never altered):** How can a vision-based mobile companion robot in MuJoCo integrate MediaPipe fall-detection kinematics to reduce emergency dispatch latency within the critical 6-minute cardiac arrest survival window for elderly individuals living alone?

**Authors:** Kamakshi Bahuguna (E007), Devanshi Sachin Kambli (B029). Faculty mentor to be listed as co-author for the conference route.

| Section | What it must say (and where the evidence is) |
|---|---|
| Abstract | Guide-specification robot, 120 seeded trials (60 falls, 60 daily activities), thresholds frozen from a 40-trial dev split: alarm sensitivity 0.68 (41/60), dispatch 0.65 (28/43), 2/77 false dispatches; 1 fixed camera 0.05, 4 fixed cameras 0.47. Slower than four cameras (median 30.4 s vs 3.8 s to the heads-up). Not safe: 25 trials where the falling person landed on it, 20 trials ended lost. Simulation only. |
| I Introduction | Long lie (Wild, Nayak, Isaacs, BMJ 1981: half of 20 people who lay over an hour died within 6 months, small cohort; Kubitza et al. 2022: one in eight older fallers lie over an hour, complications and higher hospitalisation and mortality); minutes matter in cardiac arrest (AHA 2020 guideline, Nguyen 2024). The robot controls detection, reaching and dispatch, not the ambulance. |
| II Related work | Kothari and Chakurkar 2025: YOLO plus MediaPipe, aspect ratio below 0.9, SMS alert, 96.06% accuracy. Wang and Deng 2024: BlazePose features (aspect ratio, centroid height, vertical velocity) with cascaded random forests, 89.99% accuracy, 29.7 FPS on a CPU. Chen and Duan 2021: NanoDet-Lite on a Raspberry Pi for a mobile robot. Ding and Wang 2020: device-free WiFi CSI amplitude with an LSTM. CLARA (Romero-Garces et al. 2022): socially assistive robot design, height set at 1.2 m. These use fixed or one-room viewpoints or no measured dispatch time; our contribution is the paired, seeded comparison of a mobile platform with fixed cameras, with latency from floor contact. |
| III Method | MJCF flat and guide robot (0.002 s step); FSM WATCH, FOLLOW, SEARCH, OBSERVE, SUSPECT, APPROACH, CHECK_IN, ALERT; detector (aspect ratio and its rise rate, hip descent, torso angle, quiet window); scan-matching localisation; privacy (0 bytes off-board before the first alert). Equation of motion M(q)q'' + C q' + g = tau + J^T f_ext; tip-over a_tip = g b / h = 7.7 m/s2; braking 2.4 m/s2 at mu 0.40. |
| IV Techno-economics | Dimensionless model (`analytics/healthcare_economics.py`, `analytics/csbs_business_analysis.py`): kappa, payback 12 K0 / (1 - kappa), NCRI, M/M/1 queue, Monte Carlo. Guide robot: kappa 0.45, payback 5.5 months, negative net value on clinical value alone; break-even needs about 10% of vigilance hours replaced. |
| V Results | Table 1 detection; Table 2 latency with standard errors; Figure 2 detection by zone; Figure 3 mean latency with SE bars. Welch t-test for latency (t = 7.06, df = 39.2, p = 1.7e-8, d = 1.48, robot slower). McNemar vs four cameras not significant (alarm p 0.11, dispatch p 0.15). Motion validation (500 Hz, N = 30): approach tracking RMSE median 0.022 m, mean 0.124 m. |
| VI Limitations and conclusion | Scripted motions, one flat, safety failures, assumptions in the economics (A1 to A14), 18.4 to 4.2 days and 134.5 min unsourced. Coverage, not speed, is the robot's advantage. |
